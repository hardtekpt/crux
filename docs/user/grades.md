# Grades

## Scales you log in

| Discipline | Scales |
| --- | --- |
| Bouldering | **Font** (3 … 9A) or **V** (VB, V0 … V17) |
| Routes | **French** (4a … 9c) or **YDS** (5.5 … 5.15d) |
| Either | A place's **local grades**: its own numbers or colour tapes |

Pick your scale per discipline in *Settings → Grades*. New climbs are logged in it.

## Grades are never converted

A climb keeps the scale and grade it was logged in, for good. If you switch from Font to V, your
old Font climbs still say 6C, and your bests in each scale are kept apart (see
[Progress](progress.md#hardest-sends)). No conversion is exact, and "7A" in Fontainebleau isn't
quite "V6" in a gym, so Crux doesn't pretend otherwise.

## Local grades

Many gyms grade with their own numbers or tape colours. Give a facility at a place **local
grades** (see [Places](places.md#adding-a-place)): the numbers or colours from easiest to hardest.
Climbs and problems there use them, your bests are kept per place, and a local grade is never
compared with a grade anywhere else.

## The grade converter

*You → Grade converter* is a reference for reading guidebooks and talking to other climbers. Pick a
system and a grade, and see the approximate equivalent in French, YDS, UIAA, British, Ewbank, Font
and V, with the whole chart below. It follows widely used comparison charts (Mountain Project's
international grade chart and Wikipedia's *Grade (climbing)*). British trad grades are the roughest
fit. The converter reads and writes nothing.
